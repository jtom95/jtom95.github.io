A 4 % capacitance loss, detected from the controller's own signals

Laboratory buck converter · no extra sensors · no false alarms

44 s, loops.

What you are seeing

  1. The test. The converter's output capacitance is a bank of capacitors, several of them small (about 2 % of the total each). At each check we remove one small capacitor: −2 %, −4 %, −6 %, −8 %.
  2. The data. Only the inductor current and output voltage the controller already samples for its control loop. No extra sensor is added.
  3. The estimate. At every check, the algorithm fits the converter's circuit equations to these samples and returns every physical parameter (capacitance, inductance, resistances, switch resistance) together with how certain it is.
  4. The decision. The bars show how sure we are that each part has changed since the first check. An alarm is raised at 99 %. The capacitor is flagged at the second check (−4 %, 99.2 % sure); the parts we did not touch never reach the alarm level.
The certainty is not a tuning knob: on simulated records with known truth, 95 % of the stated 95 % intervals contain the true value, for every parameter of a buck converter and a three-phase inverter.
Tomas Monopoli, Jiahong Liu, Shuai Zhao · AAU Energy, Aalborg University
T. Monopoli, J. Liu, S. Zhao, “Uncertainty-aware parameter estimation for condition monitoring of power converters,” submitted to IEEE Transactions on Power Electronics, 2026.
